Safe Project Design

We use E. coli and Pseudomonas putida as our chassis organisms, which pose no harm to individuals or environment. E. coli DH5α, DH10β, BL21, BL21(DE3), and BW25113 are utilized when we implement the experiments of molecular cloning, expression, and screening.

This year, our project aims to establish a highly efficient and versatile TnpB-based genome editing toolkit for bacterial chassis. TnpB is a compact RNA-guided nuclease and the evolutionary ancestor of Cas12. Starting from ISHahl1 TnpB, which recognizes the TAM sequence CTAC, we perform random mutagenesis of its 50–170 a.a. region to obtain variants with relaxed TAM recognition specificity. These variants are screened in E. coli BW25113 using fluorescent-reporter plasmid cleavage assays and validated by plasmid interference assays to quantify cleavage efficiency. The resulting validated TnpB variants form a versatile toolkit that enables efficient, multi-site, and programmable genome editing in bacterial chassis. To prevent the spread of engineered strains in the environment, we designed a TnpB-based suicide switch in which TnpB is programmed to target essential genes (dnaX, lspA, fabG, secY, and thiL) under an inducible promoter. TnpB expression is tightly repressed by a lab-supplied inducer in the lab; if cells escape where the inducer is absent, TnpB introduces double-strand breaks in these essential genes, leading to growth arrest and cell death. Targeting multiple essential genes simultaneously reduces the chance of escape via a single mutation.

All the proteins are totally harmless to human body. All the plasmids are harmless for human beings, and none of our parts would raise any safety issue in our project according to the current expertise.

In addition, we identified that the use of acids and corrosive chemicals in our laboratory work poses potential hazards to human health and safety. When preparing large amounts of buffered solution, hydrochloric acid used for adjusting the pH could splash out of the beaker, which could lead to corrosion of surfaces, instruments, and skin. To ensure experimental safety, all procedures involving these hazardous chemicals are conducted with appropriate protective measures to minimize the risk to both personnel and the environment.

Safe Lab Work

The laboratory is equipped with the location of emergency exits, emergency ventilation devices, fire-fighting equipment, first aid kits, and comprehensive safety management systems including personal protective equipment, inventory controls, and waste management. Every year after recruiting new members, we spend a long time training them in basic laboratory operations and handling safety accidents. When entering the laboratory, we must wear laboratory clothes, gloves, and goggles. All bacterial culture mediums have undergone sterilization treatment. We strictly follow the BSL-1 regulations to dispose of all biological materials and metabolic waste. The laboratory is also equipped with specialized operators for handling hazardous materials and biological products.

Figure 1 First aid kits of our lab.

At the same time, our PI Prof. Wenjing Hong is responsible for the safety and security of biology labs at our institution, and our institutional biosafety officer Ziyan Han ensures proper safety management for all team members.

Figure 2 Members learnt laboratory safety intentions.

During our experimental work, all procedures are conducted in a Level 1 standard microbiological laboratory. The laboratory is equipped with chemical fume hoods and clean benches to prevent contamination and ensure proper containment. We strictly adhere to the corresponding biosafety regulations. Any contaminated waste or used materials generated will be decontaminated on-site prior to further handling or disposal, in strict accordance with established waste management protocols, ensuring complete inactivation of all biological materials before leaving the laboratory. All team members who can enter the laboratory have been trained in handling the engineered bacteria.
