1 Gene editing and regulation based on ISHahl1 TnpB

1.1 General experiment of gene editing using pEcTnpB/pReRNA or pEcCas/pEcgRNA dual-plasmid system

Materials and Apparatus: -Chloramphenicol (34 mg/mL) (for pReRNA)
-Streptomycin (100 mg/ml) (for pEcgRNA)
-Kanamycin (50 mg/mL)
-20%(w/v) L-arabinose
-10%(w/v) glycerol
-1.34M L-rhamnose
-LB liquid medium (10 g/L NaCl, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L tryptone)
-LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), kanamycin (50 μg/mL) and 0.15% (w/v) L-arabinose (for pEcTnpB/pReRNA system)
LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), streptomycin (100 μg/mL) and 0.15% (w/v) L-arabinose (for pEcCas/pEcgRNA system)
-LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL) (for pReRNA)
-LB agar plate containing streptomycin (100 μg/mL) (for pEcgRNA)
-LB agar plate containing kanamycin (50 μg/mL)
-LB agar plate containing 10g/L sucrose
-LB agar plate
-E. coli MG1655/BL21(DE3)/Nissle 1917 competent cells harboring pEcTnpB/pEcCas plasmid
-pReRNA/pEcgRNA plasmid and donor fragment
-1.5 mL and 50 mL centrifuge tube
-Culture tube and shake flask
-Sterile 2 mm Gene Pulser cuvette
-96-well microtiter plates
-Takara DL5000 DNA Marker
-Tecan Infinite® M200 Pro microplate reader
-Dlab D1524R high speed micro centrifuge
-Bio-Rad MicroPulser™ electroporator
-Peiying HYG-EA multifunction combination shaker
-Cence H1750R microcentrifuge
-Heal Force TRIDENT 960 thermal cycler
-DYY-6D electrophoresis power supply
-CS Xueke ice maker

Protocol: 1. E. coli MG1655/BL21(DE3)/Nissle 1917 pre-transformed with pEcTnpB or pEcCas plasmid was electroporated with mixture of 100 ng pReRNA/pEcgRNA plasmid and 400 ng donor fragment, followed by recovery at 37 ℃ for 1.5 h. Discard the supernatant, resuspend the cells in 100 µL antibiotic-free LB medium, and spread onto LB agar plates supplemented with either: kanamycin (50 µg/mL) + chloramphenicol (34 µg/mL) + 0.15% (w/v) L-arabinose (for pEcTnpB/pReRNA system) or kanamycin (50 µg/mL) + streptomycin (100 µg/mL) + 0.15% (w/v) L-arabinose (for pEcCas/pEcgRNA system). Culture overnight at 37 ℃.
2. Screening of mutant strains. After electroporation and recovery of transformants on selection plates, individual colonies were randomly picked and verified by colony-PCR and DNA sequencing to verify the positive clones, and wild-type strains were used as controls.
3. Plasmid curing.
(1) Choose an edited colony containing both pEcTnpB/pEcCas and pReRNA/pEcgRNA and inoculate in 3 ml of LB medium containing kanamycin (50 μg/mL) and chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL, for pReRNA) or streptomycin (100 μg/mL, for pEcgRNA), The culture was incubated overnight at 37 ℃ with shaking at 220 rpm.
(2) Aspirate 1 mL of the culture, centrifuge at 6,500 rpm for 3 min, discard the supernatant, and wash the cells twice with antibiotic-free LB medium. Inoculate 2% (v/v) washed cells in 3 mL of LB medium containing kanamycin (50 µg/mL) and L-rhamnose (10 mM), and grow at 37 ℃ with shaking at 200 rpm for 6 h.
(3) Inoculate 2% (v/v) cells from step 2 in 3ml antibiotic-free LB medium and grow at 37 ℃ with shaking at 200 rpm for 2 h.
(4) Perform serial tenfold dilutions of the culture from step 3 to 10-3 and 10-4 with antibiotic-free LB medium, spread 100 µL onto a LB containing sucrose (10 g/L) agar plate, and grow overnight at 37 ℃.
(5) Pick 12 single colonies and streak them separately onto a LB agar plate、 a LB agar plate containing kanamycin (50 µg/mL), and a LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol ( 34 µg/mL, for pReRNA) or containing streptomycin( 100 mg/mL, for pEcgRNA). Colonies that growing on nonselective LB agar plate but failing to grow on antibiotic-containing plates are identified as plasmid-cured engineered strains.

1.2 Catalytically dead ISHahl1 TnpB: plasmid interference assay

Materials and Apparatus: -Chloramphenicol (34 mg/mL)
-Kanamycin (50 mg/mL)
-20%(w/v) L-arabinose
-10%(w/v) glycerol
-LB liquid medium (10 g/L NaCl, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L tryptone)
-Square LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol (34 µg/mL) and kanamycin (50 µg/mL)
-E. coli DH5α competent cells harboring pBBR1-dTnpB-TEST plasmid of different TnpB mutants and reRNA to target sfGFP or NT
-pMB1-sfGFP plasmid as target plasmid
-1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube
-Culture tube
-Sterile 2 mm Gene Pulser cuvette
-96-well microtiter plates
-Tecan Infinite® M200 Pro microplate reader
-Dlab D1524R high speed micro centrifuge
-Bio-Rad MicroPulser™ electroporator
-Peiying HYG-EA multifunction combination shaker
-CS Xueke ice maker

Protocol: 1. Inoculate E. coli DH5α carrying the pBBR1-dTnpB-TEST-series plasmids of different TnpB mutants into LB medium containing kanamycin (50 µg/mL). Grow overnight at 37 ℃, 220 rpm.
2. Subculture the overnight culture at 2% (v/v) into 3 mL of fresh LB containing kanamycin (50 µg/mL) and 0.15% (w/v) L-arabinose. Grow at 37 ℃, 220 rpm until OD600 = 0.6.
3. Aliquot the cultures into precooled 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes, centrifuge at 6,500 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃, and discard the supernatant. Repeat to harvest all biomass from each culture, then keep the pellets on ice.
4. Gently resuspend the cells in 200 µL of precooled 10% (v/v) glycerol until homogeneous. Centrifuge at 6,500 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃ and discard the supernatant.
5. Repeat step 4.
6. Resuspend the cells gently in 100 µL of precooled 10% (v/v) glycerol to get the electrocompetent cells.
7. Add 100 ng target plasmid to 100 µL of electrocompetent cells, pipette gently to mix, and transfer the suspension to a precooled sterile 2 mm Gene Pulser cuvette.
8. Set the electroporator to program EC2. Thoroughly dry the exterior of the cuvette, insert it into the instrument, and deliver a single pulse.
9. Add 1 mL of antibiotic-free LB medium to the electroporated cells immediately, pipette to mix, and transfer the suspension to a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. Recover the cells at 37 ℃ with shaking at 200 rpm for 1.5 h.
10. Centrifuge the recovered culture at 6,500 rpm for 3 min. Discard the supernatant, resuspend the cells in 100 µL of antibiotic-free LB medium, and perform serial tenfold dilutions in a sterile 96-well plate from 10-1 to 10-6. Spot 5 µL of each dilution onto square LB agar plates containing kanamycin (50 µg/mL) and chloramphenicol (34 µg/mL). Allow the spots to dry, then incubate the plates overnight at 37 ℃.
Count colonies from the highest dilution factor that yielded isolated single colonies and calculate the CFU per milliliter (CFU/mL) using the formula: CFU/mL= (number of colonies) x (dilution factor)/0.005 mL.

1.3 TnpB interference

Materials and Apparatus: -E. coli BL21(DE3) carrying the three-plasmid system: TnpB D190A (dTnpB) expression plasmid, reRNA (targeting J23118) plasmid, and EYFP reporter plasmid
-Kanamycin (50 mg/mL)
-Chloramphenicol (34 mg/mL)
-Streptomycin (50 mg/mL)
-L-arabinose stock solution (20%, w/v)
-LB liquid medium (10 g/L NaCl, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L tryptone)
-Peiying HYG-EA multifunction combination shaker
-Tecan Infinite® M200 Pro microplate reader

Protocol: Part I. TnpBi Induction with 0.2% L-Arabinose
1. E. coli BL21(DE3) carrying the three-plasmid system were cultured in LB medium supplemented with kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and streptomycin (50 μg/mL) overnight.
2. The overnight cultures were inoculated at 2% (v/v) into 10 mL of fresh LB medium containing kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and streptomycin (100 μg/mL). Three biological repeats were performed.
3. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm until OD600 reached approximately 0.6. L-arabinose was added to a final concentration of 0.2% (w/v).
4. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for a further 16 h. OD600 and EYFP fluorescence intensity (λex = 507 nm, λem = 532 nm) were measured.
5. TnpBi effectiveness was evaluated by comparing RFUEYFP/OD600 with that of the negative control.

Part II. Arabinose-Concentration Gradient Assay
The same procedure described in Part I was followed, except that the final L-arabinose concentration was varied. The tested concentrations were 0.2%, 0.02%, 0.006%, 0.002%, 0.0006%, 0.0002%, and 0.00002% (w/v), with 0% L-arabinose included as an uninduced control. Three biological replicates were performed for each gradient.

2 Directed evolution of ISHahl1 TnpB for altered/expanded TAM specificities

2.1 Selection based on fluorescence intensity

Materials and Apparatus: -Chloramphenicol (34 mg/mL)
-Ampicillin (100 mg/mL)
-20% (w/v) L-arabinose
-50% (w/v) D-glucose
-LB liquid medium (10 g/L NaCl, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L tryptone)
-LB agar plate containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), ampicillin (100 μg/mL) and 0.5% (w/v) glucose
-E. coli BW25113 competent cells harboring pEYFP plasmid of various TAM
-Tecan Infinite® M200 Pro microplate reader
-Dlab D1524R high speed micro centrifuge

Protocol: 1. 1. Prepare the error-prone PCR system according to the manual of the random mutagenesis kit, run 25 cycles, and recover the random mutagenesis product via gel extraction.
2. Prepare a 10 μL Gibson assembly system containing 5 μL Gibson enzyme, 16 ng insert fragment and 80 ng corresponding spacer backbone. Place the mixture on ice immediately after the reaction.
3. Transform 10 μL assembly product into E. coli BW25113 competent cells harboring pEYFP plasmid of various TAM. Transform pSTING-E1/2/3 plasmid as the negative control and pSTING-E1 plasmid into E. coli competent cells harboring pEYFP-origin plasmid as the positive control. After recovery, the culture was concentrated and spread onto LB agar plates containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), ampicillin (100 μg/mL) and 0.5% (w/v) glucose, then incubate overnight at 37 °C.
4. Colonies were randomly picked from plates and inoculated into LB liquid medium containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), ampicillin (100 μg/mL) in sterile culture tubes (one colony per tube), including positive and negative controls. Incubate at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 14 h.
5. Inoculate bacterial cultures at 2% (v/v) into fresh LB liquid medium containing chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and incubate at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 3 h. Set three biological replicates for each sample.
6. OD600 and EYFP fluorescence intensity (λex = 507 nm, λex = 532 nm) were measured, then 28 μL 20% (w/v) L-arabinose was added for induction for 6 h.
7. After 6 h of induction, OD600 and EYFP fluorescence intensity (λex = 507 nm, λex = 532 nm) were measured again. Relative fluorescence unit (RFUEYFP) normalized to OD600 of each sample was calculated.
8. The variants with lower RFUEYFP/OD600 than that of the negative control were selected for further experiments and analyses.

2.2 Verification of evolved variants via plasmid interference assay

Materials and Apparatus: -E. coli BW25113 competent cells harboring pSC101-TAM
-pSTING-EX plasmids of selected variants
-SOC liquid medium, with glucose replaced by 0.2% L-arabinose
-Kanamycin (50 mg/mL)
-Chloramphenicol (25 mg/mL)
-Dlab D1524R high speed micro centrifuge
-Square agar plates containing kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (25 μg/mL) and 0.2% L-arabinose (6x6 grids)

Protocol: 1. Transform 100 ng variant plasmids into E. coli BW25113 competent cells harboring pSC101-TAM. Transform pSTING-E1/2/3 plasmids as the negative control and pSTING-E1 into E. coli competent cells harboring pSC101-CTAC as the positive control. For recovery, add 1 mL SOC liquid medium in which glucose was replaced by L-arabinose, and incubate at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 3 h.
2. After recovery, the culture was concentrated to 50 μL then serially diluted tenfold (100 to 105) using SOC medium with glucose substituted by L-arabinose.
3. Spot 5 μL diluted culture onto each grid of the selective square agar plate. Two replicates for each sample.
4. Incubate at 37 °C for 24 h, followed by counting colonies.

2.3 Genome editing using evolved variants

Materials and Apparatus: -2×YT medium (10 g/L yeast extract, 5 g/L NaCl and 16 g/L tryptone)
The remaining materials and apparatus were the same as those described in Section 1.1.

Protocol: This experimental protocol was similar to the general genome editing workflow (see section 1.1) with some modifications. The variants were all cloned into pEcTnpB-SP1, in which sacB counterselection marker and reRNA targeting pMB1 were removed. All liquid culturing process used 2×YT medium (10 g/L yeast extract, 5 g/L NaCl and 16 g/L tryptone) instead of LB medium. After recovery, 75 μL culture was plated directly and the last was concentrated to plate in another plates. Colony PCR was implemented to probe the editing efficiencies of various variants. No plasmid curing process was needed during this experiment.

3 Biocontainment

Materials and Apparatus: -Chemically competent E.coli BL21(DE3) cells
-ISHahl1 TnpB expression plasmid
-Corresponding reRNA plasmids carrying spacers targeting dnaX, fabG, lspA, secY, thiL, or the non-targeting (NT) control
-Control expression plasmid retaining the L-arabinose-inducible promoter but lacking the ISHahl1 coding sequence
-Kanamycin stock solution (50 mg/mL)
-Chloramphenicol stock solution (34 mg/mL)
-L-arabinose stock solution (20%, w/v)
-D-glucose stock solution (50%, w/v)
-LB liquid medium
-LB agar plates supplemented with kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and 0.2% (w/v) D-glucose
-Shaking incubator
-High-speed microcentrifuge

Protocol: 1. Commercially available chemically competent E.coli BL21(DE3) cells were used for transformation. For the +ISHahl1 system, cells were co-transformed with the WT ISHahl1 TnpB expression plasmid and the corresponding reRNA plasmid carrying one of five target-specific spacers (dnaX,fabG, lspA, secY, or thiL) or the non-targeting (NT) spacer. For the -ISHahl1 control, cells were co-transformed with an expression plasmid retaining the arabinose-inducible promoter but lacking the ISHahl1 coding sequence, together with the matching reRNA plasmid.
2. The resulting strains were inoculated at 2% (v/v) into 10 mL fresh LB medium supplemented with kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and 0.4% (w/v) D-glucose. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for 12-16 h.
3. The overnight cultures were inoculated at 2% (v/v) into 10 mL fresh LB medium containing the same antibiotics. For each construct, two conditions were established: a condition with the survival signal, supplemented with 0.2% (w/v) D-glucose to repress the L-arabinose-inducible promoter, and a condition without the survival signal, supplemented with 0.2% (w/v) L-arabinose to induce ISHahl1 TnpB expression. Three biological repeats were prepared for each condition. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for 6 h.
4. After incubation, 100 μL of each culture was collected by centrifugation at 6,500 rpm for 3 min at room temperature. The cell pellets were washed twice with 100 μL of fresh LB medium containing the same antibiotics and finally resuspended in 100 μL of the same medium.
5. The cell suspensions were serially diluted tenfold six times by transferring 20 μL into 180 μL of fresh LB medium containing the same antibiotics at each dilution step.
6. Aliquots of 5 μL from each dilution were spotted onto LB agar plates supplemented with kanamycin (50 μg/mL), chloramphenicol (34 μg/mL), and 0.2% (w/v) D-glucose. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 16 h before colony counting.
7. The viable cell concentration was calculated as follows:
CFU/mL = (Number of colonies x Dilution factor) / 0.005 mL
Survival Ratio (log10) = log {(CFU/ml without the survival signal)/(CFU/ml with the survival signal)}
The log10 survival ratio was calculated for each spacer under both the +ISHahl1 and -ISHahl1 conditions, with and without the survival signal corresponding to D-glucose repression and L-arabinose induction, respectively.
