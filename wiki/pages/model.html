Overview

To move from system design and experimental observations to a deeper mechanistic understanding, we propose a model framework (also) called STING, standing for STructural and dynamic Insights into Nuclease architecture and Guide recognition.

Figure 1 Summary and highlight of our STING modelling framework.

Our model focuses on the IShah1 TnpB system and is organized around three interconnected directions: Structure, Build, and Expand. First, we aim to provide reliable protein-DNA-RNA ternary structure prediction with a large language model (LLM). Combining the bioinformatics tools with the reliable structure, our model provides key domain/residue information to guide the design of both base editors and engineered variants with altered TAM specificities, building the TnpB toolkit. In addition, the LLM-generated contact probabilities are employed to explain the base editor system's efficiency. Second, to accelerate directed evolution of TnpB for altered TAM specificities, our framework designs a data-driven scoring method to prioritize variants, providing not only scores but also key regions for later mechanistic analysis. Finally, we uncover the structural and dynamic mechanisms underlying guide recognition using molecular dynamics, revealing a molecular basis for future rational engineering.

Together, these three directions establish an integrated framework that connects structural/mechanistic understanding with rational toolkit design, allowing us to move from fundamental discoveries to the development of improved genome-editing technologies.
